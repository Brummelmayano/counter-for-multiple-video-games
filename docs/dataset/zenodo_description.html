PlayEnd is a dataset for detecting when a game played on a console starts and when it ends. It was recorded during 68 hours of real play in a gaming room in Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of the Congo. A Raspberry Pi took a screenshot of the console output at random intervals of 10 to 45 seconds, and large vision-language models described each of the 8,871 screenshots: the game on screen, the score, the clock, the period, the players or lives left, and so on. We marked the screenshot where each game starts, the screenshot that proves its end, and how the time on screen is billed (per game, by time, or not at all). Every start and every end was checked by hand. The 25 sessions cover 17 game titles, 303 games and 296 ends; the 7 other games were still running when the capture stopped.

The archive holds one CSV row per screenshot (observations.csv) and tables for the sessions, the games played, the segments billed by time and the cross-validation folds. It also contains the 262 encoded inputs of our recurrent model, a column dictionary in English and French, the data card (README.md) and Croissant metadata with the Responsible AI fields (croissant.json). The screenshots themselves are not included. Real player names written on screen were replaced by pseudonyms, and no calendar time is given.

Version 1.1 adds the start of each game and comes out of a second review by the first author: 650 observations and the labels of 41 screenshots changed, and one restarted fight became a game of its own. The data card lists the changes. Results on versions 1.0 and 1.1 are close but not equal; please say which version you used.
